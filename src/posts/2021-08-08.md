## From The Font

_“Stop murmuring… No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draw him”_

What Jesus is proposing in this “Bread of Life Discourse” in John 6 is nothing less than a new religion. Jesus is claiming to fulfil the “Abrahamic and Mosaic Covenant” (AKA The Old Covenant) by establishing a “new” Covenant in His own body and blood. Jesus is claiming to be, at the same time, the priest and the lamb of sacrifice…

When people around Him begin to say “this cat is crazy!!” Jesus shuts them down with an unexpected shot. In modern parlance, He says “I know what you’re thinking, but your inability to accept what I’m saying is actually a result of your weak Faith. So shuuuuush.”

In our world, we tend to feel threatened when someone disbelieves or speaks ill of one of our closely held beliefs. One need only look at social media to see how easily some people’s faith is shaken by a single quotation or rude meme. One of the most overlooked aspects of this sermon from the Bread of Life Discourse is the complete confidence with which Jesus speaks. In other sermons, e.g. the Rich Young Man, Jesus is saddened or angered by lack of faith. He isn’t especially emotional here. In fact, He seems to be expecting unbelief. The Greek word is actually “to grumble” (γογγύζετε) rather than to murmur. In many ways, Jesus isn’t saying “Shut Up!” as much as He is saying “look, it is what it is.”

The accusation that the Lord hasn’t given them the Faith to accept this hard teaching isn’t something Jesus uses all that often. It’s one of the many things about this Bread of Life Discourse that make it unique and distinct from His other, more every-day teachings. But it’s also essential to understand why this sermon is so important. This isn’t a moral teaching, which may or may not have a handful of exceptions. This is a fundamental religious aspect of the Church Jesus intends to found. It’s not something found elsewhere (like prohibitions against sexual infidelity), it’s at the heart of the Christian Faith. As such, it IS hard to absorb and hard to live out. It’s hard to believe fully with every fibre of the self that Jesus is present in the Holy Eucharist… Hence the accusation and thus the need for us to ask the Lord for that grace as often as we need it.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

It’s back to school time already!

In these strange times of ours, some things never change. Back to school is a great time for us to take stock, evaluate priorities, and to establish or reestablish routines and patterns of life. While life isn’t routine, a daily program or structured pattern is a good thing. I say that, of course, as someone with just a little more OCD than most and so I’m partial to my routines more than most. Still, the great spiritual masters of our Church all recommend certain habits of life. There are two vocabulary words that may help us to get into the right frame of mind. Those habits of life which help us make good MORAL decisions are called the VIRTUES. A pattern or structured way of living which helps us grow in love of God and in RELIGIOUS fervor is alternately called a RULE or an ORDO.

The Virtues get a lot of talk, especially from me. There are the Theological Virtues: Faith, Hope and Charity. There are the Cardinal Virtues: Prudence (Wisdom), Justice, Fortitude (Strength), and Temperance (Moderation). And there are other ungrouped virtues like humility, docility, patriotism, filial piety, etc.

The RULE or ORDO of life is too frequently considered something exclusively for religious brothers and sisters. In fact, most of us should be thinking about our daily prayers, weekly spiritual exercises, and annual religious duties as a kind of rule of life that we keep with the same certainty as we think about our daily eating, weekly and monthly personal habits, and annual medical checkups. We may not need to think consciously about personal habits like shaving or tooth brushing, but we have to plan out our lives around repeating events in our daily, weekly, monthly and yearly lives.

The return of the school day every year calls for a reconsideration of our daily and weekly lives. The beginning of a sport or an activity calls for changes in our weekly and monthly patterns. Maybe we have to plan ahead for expenses or to organize a vacation.

The same should be true of our religious lives. What does my DAILY religious life look like? Is there time for prayer? What about time for religious reading or Bible study?

What does my WEEKLY religious life look like? Is Mass a must-not-miss? Do I fast or do some kind of penance each week? Is there time for visiting the sick or doing some works of mercy?

What does my MONTHLY religious life look like? Do I go to confession? How many books do I want to read? Have I fasted in a serious way?

Finally, what does my YEARLY religious life look like? Have I made a retreat? Have I visited the pastor for a Q&A or just a check in?

Just a few minutes of serious consideration and planning can make a massive difference in our spiritual lives. Setting aside time to pray the Rosary, even in the car, could be a game changer. Going to confession regularly could be the difference between Heaven and Hell!
Back-to-school is a good time to consider other routines and habits that make for a good personal and family life.
