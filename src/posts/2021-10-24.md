## From The Font

_“Take courage; get up, Jesus is calling you”_

The notion of being especially chosen by God is built into the deepest DNA of the Jewish religon. God chose Abraham. God chose Isaac rather than Ishmael. God chose Jacob rather than Esau. God chose Joseph rather than any of his brothers. The same with Moses and Aaron and Joshua and Elijah and David and Solomon and Mary and Joseph and Peter and Paul and on and on.

There’s certainly an oddity about that in local ancient religions of the East. The gods of the non-Jewish religions of the area didn’t choose people, they were worshipped and in return for the worship, the gods gave blessings or what have you. But the Lord God wasn’t selling His blessings. God chose who He chose. He gave blessings to many - beyond the few He chose - but He chose to give His blessings THROUGH those that He chose.

Jesus, too, did a lot specifying. He picked out the Apostles, a herd of disciples, a handful of women, a slew of individual paupers and sick people to heal. He specified that this or that group (of Pharisees, Scribes, etc) had sinned and deserved punishment. He chose and He specified and He selected. And even today, unlike Islam or Hinduism, each Christian should have the sense of a personal and direct relationship with the Lord who calls each of us to specific vocations and services and duties according to the specific gifts, talents, and inclinations that He, Himself, has knit into our very beings…

The idea of a personal call to holiness goes back to the beginnings of Christianity. While the phraseology may be newish to our day and age, the notion of a personal vocation is as old as the Church.

Jesus has called each and every one of us to holiness and to a personal relationship with Him. He has also called each and every one of us to a personal attitude and disposition of service that we call a “vocation.” For most of us, that vocation is Christian family life. For some, it’s religious life or the priesthood or dedicated missionary work. Beyond that fundamental disposition, the Lord calls us day by day to specific acts of trust in Him. From visiting the sick, to personal sacrifice, to sharing a word of encouragement or admonition, to anything! God never stops calling out to us.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Thank you for all of your prayers during my retreat this past week!

Retreat is always a privileged time. Going into my retreat this year, I have been especially aware of the ongoing need to seek forgiveness of even the smallest faults and failings.

I usually try to ask forgiveness if I consciously or directly offend or hurt someone… I tend to be looser with my tongue than is charitable or prudent. But nowadays, so many of us are being slowly trained by our society to be offended by words! I find that the wisdom of my youth - Sticks and stones may break my bones, but words will never hurt me - doesn’t hold up so well.

I’ve been thinking about that for a while now… After all, the single oldest and hardest problem of all religious ministers, Christian and non-Christian alike, is “how do I deliver the hard truths that all of us NEED to hear.” It’s one thing to speak about something that’s uncomfortable like contraception or the sins of the clergy. It’s entirely another, though, to share the teachings of Jesus which are deeply misunderstood or which our society actively convolutes… The teaching of the Church, for example, on immigration is not remotely aligned to the open borders idealism of the political left in the United States. The teaching of the Church regarding people who exeperience same-sex attraction doesn’t remotely resemble much of the silliness that comes from Churches or Catholics with rainbow flags on their social media profiles. In fact, genuine charity to immigrants, migrants, and refugees is basically the opposite of what the mainstream news media portrays it to be! Showing genuine charity to those who experience same-sex attraction or gender dysphoria isn’t about unconditional acceptance, it’s the opposite. If those folks want to get to Heaven, they are called directly by scripture (Mt 19:12) to embrace their particular cross and to offer to the Lord as a powerful means of personal holiness. There’s no virtue or salvation in trying to Christianize the #YOLO hashtag or it’s cultural equivalent “I want to have my cake and eat it too!”

But boy oh boy… In our society today, these kinds of words are violence… Sticks and stones are what modern people would prescribe as the proper response to these hurtful words. And that puts all of us in a difficult spot… Jesus tells us to proclaim the truth “in season and out of season." We are definitely out-of-season at the moment!

And so I’ve just been swirling this around in my mind… Again, I want to apologize for my own faults and failures and when I’ve spoken rudely or uncharitably or imprudently or in any other hurtful way. I really am sorry. At the same time, I ask for your prayers for me and for all of us who are called by God to “say the things that men need to hear” (Eph 4:29). It’s not just the clergy who need to get their minds around the difficulties with language - it’s all of us who are charged with bringing the Gospel to all that we meet. After all, however contrary it may seem in this or that society or context, the freedom that God promises comes from His truth.

_You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free._ John 8:32
