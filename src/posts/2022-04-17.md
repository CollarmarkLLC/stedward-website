## From The Font

_“This man God raised on the third day and granted that He be visible, not to all the people, but to us, the witnesses chosen by God in advance, who ate and drank with Him after He rose from the dead.”_

In many ways, Easter Sunday is all about the idea of witness… Jesus died a brutal, public death to witness the consequence of sin. He rose from the dead in secret and appeared to a few specially chosen people until the gigantic public moment of the Ascension during which hundreds of witnesses saw Him rise into Heaven. And ten days later, the Apostles, having received the Holy Spirit as tongues of fire, began the public work of ministry as the official witnesses of Jesus’ teaching, ministry, miracles, and Church.

And we, too, are here as witnesses. We attest with our attendance and our out-of-step beliefs in the teaching, ministry, miracles, and Church of Jesus that He is God. He is the One “God raised on the third day.” And when it comes to making more Christians, it’s not usually our theology or our history or even the beauty of our Liturgy that makes converts - it’s the credibility of the witness who shares his or her faith with someone else. It’s YOUR credibility and YOUR faith which convinces someone else to give Jesus Christ a chance to bring them the “love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control” that comes naturally from putting our faith in Jesus Christ. We call these the Fruit of the Holy Spirit, but the list itself is a distant second to the witness of it…

Nothing has more impact on the life of faith than the witness of faith. Nothing builds up our faith more than seeing others around us live their faith boldly. Even supernatural events and miracles fade quickly in our minds. But the witness of someone we trust lasts.

In the same way, nothing is more detrimental to our faith than the hypocrisy or shallowness of faith of someone who professes Jesus with their lips and denies Him by their lifestyle.

As we celebrate Easter, let us be thankful for God’s goodness and His supernatural love for us which is so much more valuable than we can even begin to imagine. At the same time, let us be aware of the responsibility that flows from that gift! We are witnesses…

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

**Happy Easter!!**

Well that’s another Lenten season done. Our second “normal” Lent since the Holy Week that we had to celebrate apart.

In his sermon for the “Chrism Mass” that the Bishop celebrates with the priests of his diocese each year, our Bishop spoke about the sense of alienation and separation that has been such a part of our human experience in the past few years as something which helps us to feel all the nearer to the apostles and to Jesus Himself. After all, it wasn’t just physical suffering that Jesus endured, it was the profound sense of betrayal that makes the story of Jesus’ Passion so awful. The disciples, too, felt that way after the Lord’s death. The Gospel tells us they spent much time locked away in secret due to fear.

The Resurrection of Jesus isn’t just a big, giant deal for us who receive the grace of His mastery over death in the Sacraments, it’s also a moment of the Apostles and Mary Magdalene and of His followers in general being reintegrated into the larger community. When Jesus sends the Holy Spirit upon the Apostles, they are empowered to go out into the world again and to share in the joy and grace of communal life.

Throughout 2022, I’ve been advocating strongly for us to pay attention to our own mental health and wellness. The last few years have created a LOT of emotional disorders, even in prayerful, strong-minded, and virtuous people! One of the great gifts of Christianity is that it’s deliberately a human religion - by which I mean that God made us and He gave us a mode of worshiping Him which extends from our human nature. To “do” the Christian religion well includes “doing” human life well. And so when the Resurrection of Jesus draws the Apostles out of their isolation, it’s beneficial for them spiritually and emotionally. When Jesus asks us to pray, fast, and do works of mercy, it’s beneficial for us spiritually and emotionally. When the Lord asks us to obey His commands, to share our faith, and to be public about our religious practice, it’s beneficial for us spiritually and emotionally.

Religion isn’t just self-help. It’s much, much, more than self-help. It’s the perfection of our humanity, not merely an improvement of it.

Just like a good teacher knows the strengths and weaknesses of her student and directs him or her to the right career or field of study, so the Lord has created our religion as an extension and perfection of our human nature. It really is the greatest gift He can give in this life!

If we’re looking for happiness or a sense of purpose or an outlet to “find ourselves,” the best place to do that is within the larger context of our Faith. Shockingly many of the greatest scientists, artists, military strategists, poets, philosophers, and humanitarians in world history have been serious, faithful Catholics. That’s not accidental, it’s statistically meaningful. The Catholic Faith draws out from us the best of our humanity and puts it as the service of God and neighbor. That service gives life purpose, meaning, value and direction. And those things bring us joy, peace, love, happiness, and all of the natural pleasure that comes with them.
