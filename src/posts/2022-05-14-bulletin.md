## From The Font

_“This is how all will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another.”_

Some parts of the Gospels are tricky because they are so specifically challenging. Other parts are hard because of cultural or philosophical differences between the Biblical Jews and us. And some passages are hard to apply because they are so broad and even generic that they seem to conflict with other teachings of Jesus.

Luke 6:32, for example, says “If you love those who love you, what credit is that to you? For even sinners love those who love them.” And yet here, Jesus tells us that what is of no value is suddenly primary…

And, we have to be honest, there are more than a few moments in Scripture that work like this… They are part of the reason that we, as Catholics, don’t rely on the Bible alone for our understanding of Christianity.

To understand these apparent conflicts, we have to remember a few things. First, the Bible isn’t one book. It’s many books written to different audiences by many authors each of which was inspired by the Holy Spirit but not dictated to by the Holy Spirit. So the Biblical authors used their own talents, foibles, and best judgment to bring the Lord’s inspiration to the written word.

Second, we have to remember that in writing, the audience is the first and most important consideration!

In this apparent contradiction, for example, Jesus in John’s Gospel is speaking to the Apostles alone whereas Jesus in Luke’s Gospel is speaking to the crowd. And so it is true that loving one another isn’t necessarily good enough for all of us to think of ourselves as righteous. AND it’s true that when people look to the Bishops of the Church and see that they are in disagreement or that they dislike one another, they question whether or not these men are in fact disciples of the Lord.

There’s a reason that Biblical Scholarship is one of the hardest academic disciplines in the Church. Studying texts written across 1400 years in six or so languages isn’t easy. We, as Catholics, should read and study the Bible because the Lord speaks through it! But we should also be wary of mistaking casual or personal understanding with religious truth.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Well I am ecstatic to report that I have been reappointed by Bishop Marshall to another term of six years here in Tallulah! Honestly, the first six years flew by…

Personally, for me, this parish has been a place of profound healing after several ordeals with less-than-saintly leadership in past assignments. And I cannot express my appreciation and thanks enough to all of you for your constant love, support, encouragement, and helpful criticism!

I wrote in my letter asking the Bishop to “re-up” me that I’m a “good fit” here in Tallulah. While I’ve had to learn and re-learn some skills and I’ve had to adjust my attitude toward certain things, the mores and mindset of Northeast Louisiana fits with my way of seeing the world and I think my own quirks and idiosyncrasies aren’t too difficult for the fine people of Tallulah (and Vicksburg) to assimilate and engage.

This past weekend, I specifically asked everyone to consider writing me a letter or an email or a note or a memo or even a haiku if that’s your thing. I would like to get your thoughts on how we’re doing… Specifically, I'd like to understand what’s going well and what’s not. Are you (and your family) growing in Faith? Are you holier than you were six years ago? Are you happier? What would you like to see at Church? What builds up your faith? What knocks it down? What am I doing that is a help or a hindrance? To what should I be giving more or less priority? Are you interested in anything in the spiritual life that we’re not offering? Is something missing? Should I livestream more? Should I teach more? Is there a topic you’d like to see a presentation on?

In sum, I’m looking to make use of the added time that we have together. I understand that for most of us, our wants with regard to Church are fairly straightforward: we want Church to start on time, give us a sense of nearness to the Lord, engage us with a sermon of reasonable length and complexity, give us something practical to do and make us feel like the time we’ve spent there was worth it. We want our experiences at the Church or in the Hall to be relatively consistent and we want to know that Father is available if we need him. If I’m off-base with this assessment, I hope one of you will correct me!! But that’s what I’ve tried to do here at St. Edward. And now that we have some more time together, I’d like to see us continue to improve on what we’ve got - not with massive changes and upendings, but with incremental progress and deliberate, considered choices.

Just to be clear - I’m not now nor have I ever been of the opinion that I, as pastor, should be the one to decide on all things. Anyone who has thoughts on anything is welcome to share them. I genuinely want your thoughts, criticisms, ideas, and input. While I know St Edward and Tallulah pretty well now, we are a community that only works if we’re all open, honest and truthful.

So please consider sending me a letter or an email (or a haiku). I will keep them 100% private and confidential. I’m not looking to stir anything up, I just really want to know what you want!
