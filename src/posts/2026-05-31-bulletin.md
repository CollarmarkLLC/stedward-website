## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

We are inundated with useless noise… TicToc, cable news, podcasts, talk radio, digital billboards, and, especially, that annoying unskippable ad at the gas pump (that Tesla owners don’t have to deal with ;) - just saying…). All of that noise numbs us to any real information. The signal, as nerds say, gets lost in the noise.

There has never been a moment in human history where we have needed a way to sift the useful info from useless bits - to distinguish “fake news” and “disinformation” from what we need to know… To compound the problem, students are academically weaker than ever. Educators feel helpless. Even those of us who do read for pleasure (thank you for reading this!) are confronted with books, social posts, substacks, and links from friends that are a messy mix of AI, out of context “facts,” and dubious conclusions… Who do we believe?

The result of all this isn’t so much bad info as it is fatigue. It’s just SO MUCH EASIER to watch a vid of that cat Merv who does the Bagagwa shuffle…

In years past, priests would use this Sunday after Pentecost to give a bit of a theology lesson - a bit of something beyond Catholicism 101. But given the issues I just brought up, is that wise?. The intellectual deterioration in our culture from the time of my parents and grandparents (who valued my education and spent big on the Encyclopedia Britannica) through my own generation to Gens Z & A (whose education was stunted by Common Core and COVID) is horrifying. My generation certainly believed ourselves better and more sophisticated than the “old people” who raised us, but we could read cursive, knew who Julius Caesar was and understood that being an adult meant advancing beyond where we were at any given moment. There was a cultural conversation. There was a shared sense of religious and cultural vocabulary.

I don’t want to spit in the wind by writing about generational differences… But I want to point out that preachers, like teachers, have to address their instruction to the audience as they really are - and mine is diverse! The material that the sermon or lesson comes from doesn’t change with the audience, but the way in which it is taught does.

And so does it make sense to give a complex sermon on the essentials of the Most Holy Trinity?

1. One divine God
2. Two divine processions (of the Son and the Spirit)
3. Three indwelling but non-identical Persons
4. Four divine Relations (paternity, filiation, active spiration, passive spiration)
5. Five Notions (innascibility, paternity, filiation, spiration, procession)

Some of these words, of course, all -but-require an advanced degree in Catholic Theology (or Latin). Innascibility is “un-begotten-ness,” which I’m sure clarifies that idea nicely and completely. Still, these ideas matter.

I’m thankful for our Sunday Morning Catechism / Sunday School which gives me an excellent venue to explain these things in a way that is more accessible than “un-begotten-ness” - which is just a very, very precise way of saying that God the Father’s uncreated-ness is slightly distinguishable from God’s the Son’s uncreated-ness. And this very specific detail is unlikely to have any direct effect on whether or not any one individual in human history has gone to Heaven or not, but that doesn’t mean it doesn’t matter. All of this fundamental theology has a powerful impact as we base more and more of our belief and practice upon it. For example, Pentecostalism believes (wrongly) about this idea of the innascibility of Jesus which leads them to teach (again wrongly), that Jesus and the Father (and the Holy Spirit) are identical and hence the Holy Trinity is not God’s nature… (That’s a big whoopsie from what seems like an obscure - even irrelevant - starting point.)

But I say all of this to call to our attention just how important it is that we find a way to get some real and important info through the noise of the TicTocs and the cable news. While it may not seem all that essential to daily life, these theological ideas have profound effects and we ought to care about them and concern ourselves with them as much as we realistically can - especially in a world that doesn’t seem to be planning on quieting down any time soon.
