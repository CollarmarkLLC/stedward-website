## From The Font

_“My sheep hear my voice…”_

Nowadays, being called a “sheep” is probably an insult. Even before modern politics made the word a specific kind of insult, most of us post-industrial revolution people would hear “sheep” and think of a smelly animal valuable mostly for its wool and remarkable for its naivete.

Before the modern era separated us from the land, so to speak, sheep were incredibly important animals. They provided wool, of course. But they also provided milk, meat, and, most importantly, temple sacrifices. Sheep were highly, highly valued animals which were shepherded by people who risked their lives to protect them. Not only did the owner and the shepherd care about them, the Law of Moses and the Lord clearly cared about them, too. The Passover Supper required a lamb, as did many of the most important sacrifices performed by the High Priest in the Temple.

When Jesus refers to us as sheep, He’s not insulting us at all. He’s speaking about our own value, about God’s concern for us, and about His own willingness to lay down His life for us!

As with most of Scripture, we have to keep in mind that the Lord speaks to us in analogy. Obviously, He’s not literally calling us sheep. But neither is He extending the metaphor forever. In the case of John 10 (today’s Gospel), Jesus uses the metaphor of sheep to identify Himself with the Shepherd and, then, to identify God the Father with the owner of the sheep and then to tie Himself to God the Father by concluding with “The Father and I are one.”

For ancient hearers, the owner of many sheep rarely shepherded them. Anyone who owned a lot of livestock was wealthy and would hire someone more expendable to do the risky work of shepherding and defending his stock. Jesus specifically wants to make clear that He is one with the Lord in loving us and in caring about us and in defending us, specifically, through sacrificing Himself.

It’s really a beautiful passage, even if our modern ears are clogged a bit with some confusing metaphors and false assumptions about sheep. For what it’s worth, though, they do smell pretty rough… Just my opinion… :)

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Happy Mothers’ Day!!!

I’ll be honest, the Catholic Church has had mothers’ day for a very long time, just not on a Sunday in May… Each culture generally celebrated special days of thanksgiving and prayer for mothers, fathers, grandparents, farmers, etc on or near the feast days of the saint that they connected with that group. Farmers day, in many cultures, was on or near May 1 because it’s the feast day of St. Joseph the Worker. Grandparents day was usually around July 26 which is the feast of St. Ann, Jesus’ grandmother. Fathers’ day was usually March 19, the Feast of St. Joseph. Mothers’ day varied a lot more because there are a lot of feast days of Mary. September 8 was a very popular mothers’ day in many places as it is the Birthday of Mary. February 2 was even more popular as Mothers’ Day because it was the feast of the Purification of Our Lady. Where Christmas was not celebrated with a special festival, Mothers’ Day often filled that space. And, even though there isn't an obvious Marian feast day to point to on the second Sunday of May, it’s entirely appropriate to celebrate Mothers’ Day in May as it is the month of special devotion to Our Lady!

We have to be honest, Mothers’ Day gets more attention and more energy than Fathers Day! In part, that’s because it comes first. In part, it’s because, on average, ladies are more interested and excited by receiving gifts and being doted upon. But mostly, I think, it’s because most of us like to dote upon our moms. It’s not accidental that Mary plays such a huge roll in the life of the early Church. Jesus could very well have entered the world in a more typical family structure or He could’ve given Joseph a bigger role in the life of the Church, but He didn’t. Mary features heavily in His youth and she appears in the course of His ministry and she appears quite profoundly at His death. Beyond the Bible, The Blessed Virgin Mary has appeared to saints in Italy, Mexico, France, Portugal, Spain, Japan, Russia, and the United States just to name a few… Jesus clearly intends for His mom to play a role in salvation history.

To my mind, there’s a beautiful symmetry in the way in which God establishes His Kingdom on Earth as a kind of perfection of our own experiences in family and personal life! In order to teach us about the Most Holy Trinity, the Lord orders us in families. In order to prefigure the role of the Blessed Virgin in our lives, He gives us mothers. In order to help us relate to God the Father, He gives us fathers. And so it is with all the aspects of our lives - the ups and the downs, the good and the bad - that the Lord uses what we know to reveal to us that which would otherwise be beyond our comprehension.

It’s both humbling and amazing then, that God would use us - you and me - to reveal Himself to other people just because we are parents or children or mothers or fathers. How remarkable!

To all of our mothers and expecting mothers, I wish you a very, very happy mothers’ day and to all of those whose mothers have gone on to their reward, let us pray for them and be thankful for them. To those whose mothers are still here - you’d better make this year a good one!
