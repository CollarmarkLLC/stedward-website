## From The Font

_“The wicked say: Let us beset the just one, because he is obnoxious to us; he sets himself against our doings”_

Throughout history people have asked, as did Rodney King, “Can’t we all just get along?” The short answer is “no, we can’t.” Human nature precludes us from “live and let live” and just getting along with one another… No matter how innocuously or quietly we might want to go about our lives, there will always be someone who wants to control us simply because they can. There is no such thing as a happy medium. There is no such thing as “you do you.”

The entire Old Testament is one long story of the Jews seeking peace on their own terms. They tried to find peace by isolating themselves from others. They tried to find peace through conquering and military strength. They tried strong government, religious government, submission to others, Kings, Judges, ancestor rule, etc. They tried everything they could think of and everything failed. Superficially, we might see that failure as a bad thing, the Jews certainly did! BUT, the Lord often has to remind us that He is God and we are not. The Jews didn’t understand that God’s intent was to establish a supernatural domain of eternal life. Now that we know about Heaven, we can see the discomforts and evils of the world not as ends in themselves, but as means to sanctify us and to prevent us from becoming so attached to this life that we lose eternal life.

The modern world, rife as it is with luxury and illusion\lies, calls us to a blend of naive optimism and materialistic tunnel vision. We are encouraged to worry about the minutiae of this life and to maximize our happiness and to minimize our unhappiness. We’re encouraged to obsess over our social status, our physical beauty, our personal gifts and personal weaknesses. And while that may bring out the good in some, it will, without question, bring out the bad in many. That kind of living encourages the vices rather than the virtues and it distracts us from our salvation which has little or nothing to do with social status and the specific gifts God has given us.

As Jesus says, in the world, you will have troubles, but I give you my peace! Let not your hearts be troubled, trust in Jesus and He will give you peace!

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

This week we have our usual Parish Council meeting on Tuesday and our monthly Wednesday Evening Catechism talk on Wednesday. Thursday is the feast of Padre Pio, an Italian monk with a pile of supernatural (aka “mystical”) charisms (aka “dramatic spiritual abilities”). As such, I’ve titled the Wednesday Evening Catechism talk “St. Francis of Assisi, Padre Pio, and all those crazy mystics with their supernatural gifts.”

I’ve always been fascinated by the diversity of Christian saints. Since I first began to take my faith seriously in High School, I’ve read story after story about this or that saint. This one was a spoiled little rich kid who got his act together one day and founded a religious order (St. Ignatius of Loyola, St. Francis of Assisi). That one was a saint from a young age who saw angels, advised other saints, read souls, and healed miraculously (St Rose of Lima). This one was a mean old codger with a heart of gold and a brilliant mind (Jerome). That one was unambitious and became a saint through the simple work of love and prayer (St. Jean Vianney, St. Andre Bessette). And there are others! Saints who could read minds, saints who could heal with a touch, saints who could physically fly, saints who could be in two physical locations at once, saints who had the physical wounds on Jesus, saints who could see angels, saints whose bodies haven’t decayed in the centuries since they died…

Much like the unexpected and yet deeply mystical and deeply real aspects of our Faith (Holy Water, Relics, etc), the sheer breadth of mystical gifts and the wide range of types of people to whom they were given is a testament in itself to God’s grandeur and His wisdom.

A number of theologians and Churchmen that I deeply respect have argued that the greatest weakness of the Church right now is the lack of emphasis on the supernaturality of our Faith. Catholics believe that Jesus Christ Himself enters miraculously into plain bread and wine at every Mass! We believe in Sacramental Confession and that Baptism fundamentally marks the soul! These aren’t symbolic acts, they’re supernatural! And yet most of us (and most visitors) don’t really see much difference between what we do at Sunday Mass and what the Baptists or the Methodists do. Other than the different order of events, I doubt most Americans could tell the difference… That’s a pretty big problem!

One of the biggest reasons I’m such an advocate for the Traditional Latin Mass is that it’s designed to reinforce the supernaturality of what we do at Mass while the new Mass (of 1968) seems designed to underplay it… But that’s not the point of this article.

The talk on Wednesday will highlight some of the major spiritual gifts that have been given to various saints in history by means of some fun and wondrous stories. Padre Pio is certainly among those who I’ll be highlighting and so those in attendance will be able to attend Mass in his honor the very next day… neat!

The talk will be livestreamed Wednesday morning at about 9:30a for those unable to join us in the evening.
