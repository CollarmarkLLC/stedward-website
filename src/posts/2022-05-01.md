## From The Font

“When Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he tucked in his garment, for he was lightly clad, and jumped into the sea…”

“Jesus said… Amen, amen, I say to you, when you were younger, you used to dress yourself and go where you wanted; but when you grow old, you will stretch out your hands, and someone else will dress you and lead you where you do not want to go.”

Normally, we wouldn’t see these two parts of today’s Gospel as connected. The translation can be misleading. The important connection is actually in Peter’s wardrobe… No really! First, we need to know that the ancient Jewish equivalent of sweat pants was just a tunic that was secured by a belt. It could be converted from a loose gown to something like coveralls with clever folding and rearranging the belt.

So, when Simon Peter begins today’s Gospel, he’s “lightly clad.” That means he was wearing some type of underpants and was not wearing his tunic or his belt. Dressed like this, he swims to Jesus.

Then, Jesus confronts Simon about what happened in the High Priests’ Courtyard and asks Simon three times “do you love me?” Simon answers “yes” with intensity. Then Jesus says, “you used to ~dress~ GIRD yourself and go where you wanted but \[now\]... someone else will ~dress~ GIRD you and lead you where you do not want to go.” To Gird, is to strap on a belt or a restraint (like chains).

Simon begins without a belt binding him to any obligation. But the full proof of his love of Jesus will be shown when Peter is bound with chains and led to Caesar in Rome to be Martyred!

For Peter, this message is very specific and intense. But we, also, hear these words of Jesus. If we love the Lord, we have to be willing to be led by Him into parts of our lives that we might prefer to avoid… If the Lord asks me to share my Faith, I may prefer not to, but if I love the Lord, then I have to be willing to be led “where \[I\] do not want to go.” If I love the Lord, then I have to be willing to let someone else GIRD me and I have to be willing to let myself be bound to DOING what the Love of Jesus in my Heart demands.

I love these weird little details of the Bible!

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

It’s a big week for eight of our young Catholics!

On Sunday, Harper Bedgood, Travis Hamilton, Thomas Myers Hamilton, and Gabriel Paz will receive First Holy Communion!

Then, on Wednesday, Preston Collins, Henry Elerbee, Caroline Marsh, and Chandler Wood will be confirmed by Bishop Robert Marshall!

The Sacraments of Initiation are certainly about becoming fully a part of the Catholic Church. But that’s easy to misunderstand as something akin to becoming part of a social club or a sorority… The Catholic Church is the mystical body of Jesus Christ on Earth. Membership in the Church isn’t about dues or opportunities, it’s about union with Jesus and about access to the Sacraments.

What our young people experience this week is a visceral reminder to each of us that our membership in the Church is no small matter! Being part of the Church isn’t about being on a list somewhere or getting customized envelopes or a birthday card. It’s about Grace! It’s about Sacramental Grace. As a part of the Church, we benefit from the prayers of many souls. We benefit from the example of the Saints and of the local community. We benefit from access to the Holy Eucharist and to Holy Communion, to Confession, and to Anointing of the Sick.

We also benefit from the duties and obligations which are given to us by the Lord not as a burden, but as a clear direction and purpose. In the commands and teachings of Jesus, we are directed toward our highest human good and we are protected from our human faults and from demonic attack. In the moral teaching of the Church, we’re relieved of the need to consider all matters for ourselves and we’re relieved of the conceit that we would even be able to!

Even beyond these benefits of being part of the Church, there’s the supernatural, Sacramental Grace that comes from incorporation into Christ. There are so many things we can’t perceive in our limited lives on Earth. We can’t understand the spiritual warfare happening all around us. We can’t comprehend the effects of sin and the damage it causes. We can’t know how much good comes from my choice to act on the little impulse the Lord puts in my heart and we can’t know how much damage comes from my fear to act on it. As part of the Church, though, we are instructed and directed by the Liturgy and the Catechism and the gentle movement of the Holy Spirit in hearts activated by a good sermon or a blessed moment of prayer or a reading at Mass or even a random comment from someone.

Finally, we can’t ignore the fact that the Lord uses people of all sorts, but that it’s easier for us to perceive Him at work when we’re thinking about him! Just as reading a book about healthy lifestyle improves physical health, thinking about the Lord and attending Mass regularly and confessing frequently improves our spiritual health. The Church improves that even further by assuring us of true spiritual things rather than mere pop-spirituality…

And so congratulations to all our young people who gain these benefits this week!
