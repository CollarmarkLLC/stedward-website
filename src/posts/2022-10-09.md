## From The Font

_“Blessed is the womb that carried you… Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God and observe it.”_

For most of the ancient world, including the Jews, religion was a matter of family. Various gods were believed to act as patrons of cities, families or nations. The Jews understood themselves to be God’s Chosen People.

Even today, more and more, the rise of woke culture has actively reasserted that race, skin color, etc confer a moral righteousness without any reference to choices or decisions. Of course, this is Marxist nonsense… But it’s entirely compatible with the barbaric paganism of the ancient world.

Jesus rejects this entire mode of thinking and says that the choices we make, especially those choices which flow from love of God and love of Neighbor define righteousness or moral goodness (which is translated here as “Blessed”).

The impact of this teaching is incredibly important. For one, just think about the simple fact that in the ancient world, you couldn’t just convert from one religion to another. Only a very small number of folks who weren’t born as Jews were able to become Jews. Almost entirely as a function of marriage. But in our Christian Faith, literally no one can become Christian without converting (even in infant baptism we have the Godparents profess Faith on behalf of the child).

This principle of deliberate choice to convert and follow Jesus rather than the principle of family patronage remains relevant today, especially in light of the many, many efforts to revive ancient pagan traditions under the guide of enlightened atheism.

Of course, the other thing to remember is that once we’re a part of the Lord’s family, we can’t just rest on our laurels! We have to continue to hear the word of the Lord and observe it! Unlike ancient religions, Christianity isn’t a one and done sort of thing. My holiness, my happiness, and my salvation are mine to gain or lose little by little with each choice I make in my day. Will the next choice be an expression of my Faith? Will I do as the Lord asks?

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

The idea of “divine patronage” goes back to the very beginning of human history and culture. Just about every city, army, and important family had a patron god of some sort. Even the Jews - who were monotheistic - claimed the One True God as their patron and considered themselves the Chosen People.

When Jesus established the Church on the person of Peter the Apostle, He chose not to mention quite a lot about the practical spiritual life that Christians would embrace. Other than saying that Christianity would be the completion and perfection of Judaism, Jesus didn’t mention much practically about public prayer, private prayer, fasting, penance, sin and reconciliation, anointing of the sick, marriage, funerals, baptisms, relics, holy water, blessing objects, blessing people, the priesthood, bishops, consecrated religious, chaplets, chapels, church buildings, community management, financial management, etc. And He didn’t mention what, if anything, was to be done with the title Chosen People and the Jewish, now Christian idea, of Divine Patronage.

The Apostles were told, in no uncertain terms, that they were in charge and they needed to figure out what they needed to figure out… They imitated Judaism in a lot of their early decisions and when something arose that they didn’t know how to deal with, they prayed, discussed, and acted.

One of the earliest question marks was to do with relics. All over the world - from Spain to Rome to India - the bodies of martyred Christians were being associated with miracles… Judaism didn’t offer much insight in that regard and so the apostles prayed, discussed, and eventually used the idea of divine patronage as a way of thinking about what would become the theology of the “Communion of the Saints.”

Our patron, St. Edward the Confessor, is a kind of poster child for this way of thinking. He was a holy guy and set an excellent example of leadership, piety, support for the Church, and being an attentive father to his children. But that’s not why he was canonized. He was canonized because miracles - lots of miracles - began to happen to people who went to Westminster Abbey to pray at his tomb. Even though Edward’s dynasty was ended with the military blunder of his son at Hastings in 1066, the new ruling family couldn’t ignore all the miracles! And so it was that Edward because St. Edward.

Many modern Protestants criticize the theology of the Communion of Saints as unbiblical, and they're mostly correct. But every miracle that has taken place since about 85AD is unbiblical! But Jesus told us that believers in Him would do greater works than He did (Jn 14:12)... hence the Church and the opportunity to add to and clarify the limited explanations provided by the Lord.

Patronage is an odd part of our Faith to be sure, but it’s a very real thing. All we need to do to have a patron saint is to ask that saint to pray for us. And all we need to do to take advantage of the Communion of Saints is to ask for those saints to pray for us. It really is that simple.
