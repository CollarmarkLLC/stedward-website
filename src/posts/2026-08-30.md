## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Satan is a name we don’t hear much anymore. Nowadays many scholars and even some clergy TRY to argue that he’s not real. But: “The devil’s greatest success was to convince the world that he does not exist.” An enemy who doesn’t know he is at war is an enemy already beaten.

The Gospel tells us that to do the work of Satan, we don’t have to sacrifice animals and light black candles. Satan’s way is simpler: Jesus tells Peter *You are thinking not as God does, but as men do.*

Are all of us then being used by Satan? Certainly not! But when we start to decide that my way of thinking trumps God’s then things don’t go well. We end up deciding that the Church’s is just one opinion among many and that being a Christian is whatever I want it to be. It’s when we get into this kind of thinking that we give the devil room to move - not necessarily to possess us - but to convince us that we know better than God. To stroke our egos. To build up our pride. To tell us that serving or kneeling is for the weak and not the “spiritually mature” people like me.

Why talk about Satan at all, though? First, to combat his successful advertising campaign and to assure you, from personal experience, that Satan, the devil, is not a fairy tale, is not a myth, is not a joke or a movie, Satan is real and is active in this country and in this world. Second, to make sure that we all know that there is a spiritual battle taking place and it is not small and it is not confined to monks and nuns.

The thing is, when we confront that reality honestly and directly, we have no need to fear. The battle is already won. Our concern should be to stay on the winning side - not to pretend the battle isn’t taking place. The Lord is with us!

Paradoxically, the Lord tells us the way to victory is not through power or force of arms, but through the humble acceptance of suffering and trials in this world… How bizarre! Satan is the cause of a lot of our struggles…

It’s human greed, for example, inspired and compounded by the devil that makes it hard for us to make ends meet. It’s human arrogance and the desire for power that make our political systems unjust. It’s human lust and jealousy, encouraged by the devil, that cause baseless troubles in our marriages and relationships. It’s human self- interest, perhaps the devil’s greatest tool, that causes us to miss or ignore the needs of others - even those we love. The devil is the reason that we struggle to overcome our human nature through the teaching of Jesus and the Grace of the Holy Spirit…

And despite all of that, the Lord tells us that our victory over the devil is to be found not with a weapon or even an Angel, but by taking up our Cross and following Jesus in His Passion and suffering.

If we didn’t have living examples from our daily life of how adversity strengthens us (sports training, overcoming bullies, etc) then we would be hard pressed to believe these words of Jesus. But human nature cannot hide itself from us. We men and women are hard wired to grow from difficulty and hardships. We know that luxury is dangerous to us. There’s a stunning amount of evidence that is continuously being gathered about just how unhealthy - physically and mentally - comfortable living is for us. Fasting, it turns out, is absolutely essential for physical health. So is strenuous exercise and exposure to cold and heat. We need mild sunburns. Refined sugar is apparently worse for us than smoking. Social Media may be the single most toxic drug ever created.

And the clear response is simply to live in less luxury… Fast on a regular basis… Eat real food that isn't processed. They won’t taste as good, but that’s ok. Because every little suffering - even something as small as passing on a spoonful of sugar with your coffee or a candy bar - is a small opportunity to take up our cross and be that much nearer to Jesus on the Cross. As Mary said at Fatima, we should offer up these little sufferings for our sins and in reparation for the sins of the world.

As with all truly human things, taking up our cross is not merely necessary for us spiritually. It turns out, and both science and history agree, that it’s necessary for us mentally, emotionally, and physically. It’s necessary for us as individuals, as friends, as families, and even as a broader community…
