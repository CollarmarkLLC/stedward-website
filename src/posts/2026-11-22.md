## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

At first glance, today's Gospel is a pep-me-up. Most of us are the kind of people who try to help folks. Maybe we think twice about driving past the buskers on street corners asking for money or about sending away folks looking for day labor... But most of us, I expect, see ourselves as ending up on the Lord's right among the sheep and not ending up with the goats.

But that's a super-serious assumption to make… Perhaps even more than the specific criteria Jesus gives us in the Gospel, the Lord is telling us that there is a REAL judgment that comes after we die. It's not a ticket in Tensas parish that you just need to call to get fixed… The Lord specifically says that plenty of people are going to assume they’re good to go and are going to be shocked to find that they're not.

And more than specific things like visiting the sick or feeding the hungry, the very fact of a real judgment should be a driving force in our lives…

The Gospel last week, after all, featured the young girls, half of whom were left in the lurch when their lamp oil ran low...

We have to remember that the original sin of Adam and Eve was to trust in themselves to decide right and wrong. They said to themselves that I should be able to eat the fruit of this tree, regardless of what commandments God gave…

It’s easy to convince myself that I have a good excuse for this or that? Especially if I am more or less a good person by the relatively mild standards of this modern world of ours. It’s even easier as social media has created this “performative” ideal of our public personas with their insta-filters and easy approval of whatever social cause is recommended by the algorithm.

But then Jesus says there is a REAL JUDGMENT. And that real judgment is awful black and white and it doesn't for a second mention common sense, the general opinion of society, social media stats, or the long list of excuses that the *goats* have for failing to recognize Jesus in the poor or to obey His commands or to substitute God's commands with their own.

The real concern for most of us is not that we’re evil, terrible people. The REAL DANGER is that we’ll be content with being morally FINE... with doing basically what everyone else is doing in terms of morals. With trusting in our own intuition and not keeping our eyes fixed on Jesus - who is the way, the truth, and the life!

The easiest way to see where you stand on that is to ask when was the last time you went to confession?

Confession only makes sense if I believe that Jesus - not His teaching, but Jesus Himself - is Lord. We go to confession to humble ourselves, trusting that Jesus intends to act through His Church in the sacraments. And that belief - that Jesus intends to act through His Church is what has to drive our moral choices...

Common morality or courtesy is that kind of morality which makes us act in a way that is acceptable in this or that situation. It’s performative. Christian morality, though, isn’t just a more restrictive version of common morality, it’s a set of choices we make because we believe in the Lord Jesus, We trust the Lord Jesus, and we love the Lord Jesus.

And that’s why we confess... Not because we broke a rule, but because a moral failure - a sin - is actually a failure in faith, hope, and love. It’s a failure to be who Jesus wants me to be.

And that’s ultimately why being moral isn’t enough. Morality is just an expression of our belief, trust and love of Jesus.

Why is it a sin to miss Sunday Mass? Because that Sunday worship is essential for us to know, love and serve the Lord.

Why should we go to confession? Because Jesus specifically established the Church for our salvation.

All of these choices come back to trusting Jesus and Jesus is not only the judge, but the criteria by which we are judged. He is what all of this is about. And the degree to which what we do is about Him, is the degree to which it matters in terms of judgment and eternity.
