## From The Font

_“...the more He ordered them not to, the more they proclaimed it…”_

Even theologians ask the question of why Jesus was so adamant that everyone keep quiet about the miracles He did all over the place… It feels both counter-productive and oddly futile. That confusion gets worse when we switch from one of the “synoptic” Gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke) to the Gospel of John where Jesus seems all-too-happy to share the stories of His miracles with any- and everyone around Him.

The two keys to understanding this mystery are simple enough. First, the four Gospels were written to specific audiences and across several decades. Mark was probably written in the 50s or early 60s AD to Christians in Rome. Matthew and Luke were both written in the late 60s or early 70s AD to Jewish and Greek believers around what we would call the Middle East. All three were written to help convert non-Christians to the Gospel of Jesus. St. John’s Gospel came much later, probably in the 80s AD and it was written mostly for the Christians of modern-day Turkey. So it wasn’t meant to convert people, but to strengthen the Faith of people who were already Christians… Hence the difference between the four Gospels.

The other key is found in Jesus’ interactions with Herod during the night of His Passion. Herod wants to see a miracle and Jesus refuses. Why? Because, as Jesus will later tell Thomas, it is better to have not seen and yet believed. Herod wanted religious entertainment (as do many folks in our own day and age). Jesus worked miracles to build Faith, not to entertain. He wanted His miracle kept quiet so as to keep the focus on the Faith and to reinforce the truth that Faith is not about sensation, but about supernatural relationship… If miracles get in the way of the fundamental aspects of Christianity, then they are, by definition, distractions and detriments to Faith rather than confirmations and encouragements to it.

The Lord’s goal is not to make this life into a Hallmark movie, but to give us the tools that we need to enter into eternal life with Him in Heaven. The Lord is working to that end with each of us, but our own differences and our shared experiences of the different ways in which God goes about that work are sometimes best kept quiet or even invisible to avoid confusion.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

This Wednesday, Sept 8 is the feast of Our Lady’s birthday. My first parish as pastor was Nativity of the BVM in Campti near Natchitoches and so I have a special love for Her feast day.

Tradition gives us all sorts of interesting and quirky little details from the name of the Angel that stopped Abraham from killing Isaac through to the date of Our Lady’s birth and the fact that Joseph was a widower who had several children before he was engaged to Mary. These little details aren’t going to get anyone to Heaven any faster, but they fill in the gaps and provide color to stories that can seem rather bland for the inspired Word of God…

We have to remember that the Gospels and, indeed, most of the Bible, wasn’t written like an encyclopedia or a modern history textbook. The authors of the Books of the Bible were limited both by their specific purpose in writing and by their circumstances. My purpose in these bulletin articles is specifically to bring to you interesting details of our Faith that don’t necessarily fit into a sermon or a lesson. Maybe they’re too small or maybe they just don’t matter all that much, but they are interesting and fascinating to me and I hope they’ll also be interesting to you.

My circumstances in writing these bulletin articles are wildly different from the ancient authors! I can write as many words as I want. I can change and misspell and respell. I can look up dates and names and quotations instantly. I can print 40 copies in the time it would’ve taken a Biblical writer to sharpen his quill and condition his scroll.

When we think about what details were and weren’t included in the Book of Genesis (The Archangel Uriel) or in the Gospel of St. Luke (Mary’s Birthday), we have to remember that those texts were written in longhand using expensive materials and so the author needed to have a very clear picture of why he was writing and what details he needed to include to accomplish that purpose before ever setting pen to paper.

If I write a stinker of an article, it’s no harm, no foul. I’ve wasted some of my time and some of yours, but that’s the worst of it. If an ancient author wrote junk, it was a real issue. Even as recently as the modern era, American poet Emily Dickinson wrote most of her poetry on scraps of paper despite the relative wealth of her family…
That context matters when we think about Biblical principles like Martin Luther’s ‘sola scriptura.’ Luther’s idea that ONLY the words recorded in the Bible are truly revealed by God is absurd… Neither can he argue that the Bible has ALL that is needed for salvation because that principle itself is not to be found in the Bible. Nor can he argue that the Bible itself is necessary at all because the Bible wasn’t compiled until the 400s and it wasn’t broadly available in vernacular translation until the Gutenberg Press in the 15th century and was not available cheaply until the last 200 years. Fascinating little details…

As important as the Bible is, there’s a lot more to Christianity than just the Bible… Some of it is important, some of it is just interesting.
