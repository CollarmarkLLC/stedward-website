## From The Font

_"Are you the King of the Jews?"_

Kingship for the Jews is quite different from the kingship of the other Eastern cultures of what we call the Middle East and barely even the same idea of the “modern” kingships of Louis XIV of France or Queen Elizabeth.

Jewish Kings were appointed by God and anointed for their work by prophets or chief priests. The King was meant to be a moral icon for his people and to be the “vicar” (one who “stands in the place of”) for the people as a whole. He certainly had some political and economic responsibilities and he had a lot of military authority, but the king wasn’t what we think of as kings now.

In the specific time in which Jesus is asked by Pilate if He is king of the Jews, the kingship of Israel was even less because of the Roman occupation. The king of the Jews at the time of the Romans did little more than function as a propaganda machine for Rome. The Romans had a word for it, but in the vernacular, we call it a “puppet regime.” King Herod lived rich and fat and proclaimed as law whatever Caesar’s governor told him to say. That was it.

When Jesus was asked by Pilate if He is king of the Jews, He demurred. Jesus was born into the royal line of David and Jesus was the moral icon for His people and He really was the vicar for the entire Chosen People. And so in all the ways that really matter, He was the King of the Jews.

On the other hand, Jesus was not the puppet king and He was not coming to establish a new military regime against the Roman occupiers as many expected or hoped of the Messiah.

Jesus was a true king and yet not the kind of king that the Jews expected or wanted. That tension was true of most of what Jesus said and did. As a teacher (“Rabbi”) and as a preacher (Prophet), Jesus recalled the original plan of God in scripture and in Human Nature and He decried the twisting of each in the practice of Judaism that had become the norm. It only makes sense, then, that the same would be true of His real identity as the true King of the Jews.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

We usually call today’s feast by it’s short-form name: “Christ the King.” As Americans, though, we have a distaste for kingship and royalty. The full title of the feast is “Our Lord Jesus Christ, King of the Universe.” That also has the potential to raise hackles for us as Americans because it doesn’t leave much room for the multiculturalism that is close to all our hearts.

While the woke nonsense and the idol of diversity that is espoused by our media is just foolishness, the American ideal of the Melting Pot is an icon of our strength. When we, as American Catholics, though, celebrate Jesus as King of the Universe, we celebrate the truth that Jesus is Lord of all… He’s Lord of Catholics and Baptists and Muslims and Buddhists and atheists and even those who make a religion of their politics. We’re celebrating that His truth is absolute across the entire universe.

Some years ago, I preached a sermon and I mentioned that the Hindu deities aren’t real… There is no Shiva and Vishnu just as there is no Hermes and Zeus and no Thor and Odin… I was accosted after Mass by a well-meaning lady who called me a bigot for imposing my beliefs on others and loudly explained how she had Hindu friends and loved to worship with them. I asked if she was Catholic… She was, so I offered to hear her confession because she was in a state of mortal sin for worshipping false idols. She stormed off. Hopefully, the Lord turned her heart!

We all have friends who believe wrongly. “All have sinned and fallen short of the Glory of God!” All of us human beings are fragile! That doesn’t give us license to invent our own truths - that’s just me inventing my own religion. And, of course, a lot of us do that too!

As Christians, we believe that Jesus told the truth (the Virtue of Faith). Part of the truth that Jesus told is that there is one Lord, one God. God, and God alone, exists eternally as three Divine Persons (Father, Jesus, Holy Spirit) and that all that exists is their creation. Period. End of sentence! And so the Muslim concept of God is false. The most common Pentecostal notion of God is wrong. The common idea among Baptists that salvation can be gained by answering an altar call is no more real than reality TV.

That doesn’t mean that we as Catholics should walk around wagging a finger and pretending that we don’t sin! It doesn’t mean we can help anyone by beating them up or calling them out… There is a place for sharing the truth of Christianity with those who have a faulty worldview… There is a real moral duty for all of us (not just priests) to believe that loving others means sharing the whole truth of Christianity (which is the Catholic Faith) with them. But the practical strategies of evangelization are secondary to the reality of what we believe in Faith about Jesus.

Jesus is the King of the Universe. He is Lord of all. There is no way to believe what we believe and also to make room for someone else’s concept of God or gods. And while that can be uncomfortable for us as Americans, it’s an essential and absolutely mandatory part of our beliefs as Christians and as Catholics.
