## From The Font

_“You are the salt of the earth… You are the light of the world…”_

In one sense, these words of Jesus are very, very simple. He is sending the apostles into the world as a transformative force. Salt, at the time, was used to make food more nutritious and longer lasting. Then, as now, it was one of the primary flavors of all food. Light, at the time, was a precious commodity. In a world without candles and where oil was expensive, the primary source was natural. But when God turned out the lights at sunset, oil lamps were all that remained. Jesus sent His apostles to enhance and better the world. And He used images that were easy for everyone to understand.

But in another sense, these words of Jesus are quite profound. Salt, then as now, doesn’t so much change the flavor of the food as it makes the food taste more like itself. Science might reference taste receptors on the tongue, but any home cook knows that salt “brings out the flavor” more than it brings its own flavor to the food. Light, too, doesn’t change the room that it illuminates so much as it makes the room look more like itself. A well lit room isn’t different than a poorly lit room, but the decor is more pleasing to look at, the art is more moving, the colors more effective, and the conversation all the better because of it.

Christianity IS a force for change in the world. But Jesus’ metaphors here have especially potent meaning when we realize that He appoints US to go into this world (which is our world) and make it what it can be… what it should be… what the Lord made it to be!

Certainly, there are things which are just bad… Landfills and un-recycled batteries and plastic in rivers are just bad. That stuff needs to be cleaned up and disposed of… But much of the world doesn’t need to be destroyed so much as it needs to be reminded of its purpose, mission, and true nature.

In this lifetime, Christianity has to be oriented first toward salvation and then toward making the world into what God created it to be - a living embodiment of His love for us. Salt makes food taste more like itself. Light allows us to see things as they are with clarity and detail. As Christians, we have the same missions in regard to the truths of eternity and the truths of this present age.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

After Masses on Sunday, Fr. Sklar and I will be heading to the small town of Hanceville, Alabama to begin a week-long retreat.

The Church’s official code of law (AKA Canon Law) requires every priest to make a retreat every year. It doesn’t recommend one or even strongly encourage one… It requires the priest to make a retreat every year.

The primary reason for that in history is that priesthood is a full time gig and there needs to be a deliberate pause for the priest to reset himself and reorient himself to the Lord. Nowadays, though, the retreat is all the more important and all the more difficult to find…

Priesthood even 75 years ago, was pretty straight-forward. Unless the priest was a missionary or he lived in a place of religious persecution, priesthood was a pretty cushy way to live. ...Get up every morning, say a Mass or two, visit the school, drop by the homebound, lunch at the rectory, and then maybe a meeting or two in the evening… Even in a small parish, the administration and business affairs were on the shoulders of the trustees or the diocese. Priests back then were thoroughly trained in seminary and while society around him may have had moral question marks to consider, the local priest was the teacher and preacher of divine truth.

Nowadays, though, the priesthood is more akin to a water ride at a sketchy theme park in the height of summer! Priest training in seminaries is hopelessly limited. That’s not because the seminaries don’t try - it’s because a priest these days isn’t “just” a teacher and preacher of divine truth. Modern priests, and especially modern pastors, are expected to defend the teachings of the Church rather than just teach them. Those teachings are being denied by the news, by other priests, and even by bishops on Twitter and on TV almost every day. We’re also expected to know the language of modern communication and to teach in a way that reaches Baby Boomers and Millennials and GenX and GenZ and non-English speakers and folks who drive in from elsewhere and non-Catholic relatives alike.

The priest is also expected to be a businessman with excellent financial management skills. He needs to have top notch people skills. He needs to be an armchair psychologist, an amateur social worker, a decent party planner, a small-scale logistics manager, a toastmaster, and he needs to be able to make sense of however the news is reporting the latest from Pope Francis…

I’m certainly not complaining, mind you! I love being a priest and I love being the pastor of St. Edward - but I also want to make clear just why the Church says that a retreat is necessary and not just helpful.

Because on top of all of this practical stuff, the modern priest\pastor is also expected to be a disciple… And a living embodiment of Jesus on the ground in his parish… His parishioners want him to be a saint. And they deserve nothing less!

But sanctity takes time, effort, and long-proven spiritual exercises like retreat. So, I hope you’ll pray for me and for Fr. Sklar and for all our priests that they can keep their focus amidst the present chaos. Come Holy Spirit!
