## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

O ye of Little Faith...

Just in this short story, Peter has endured a frightening storm, encountered Jesus in the guise of a ghost, spoken to said ghost, stepped out onto the choppy water, and walked toward Jesus in the guise of this ghost.

From my point of view, that’s a whole lot of faith. Probably more than I can muster.

In fact, all of the apostles were men of substantial faith. They had all left everything they had to follow Jesus without fully understanding what His plan was.

It’s clear that some of them - like the Sons of Zebedee - believed that He was a messiah who was going to rule as a king.

Others, like Judas - seemed to think that He had a much less certain future and that Jesus needed to play His cards right if He wanted to succeed.

None but Peter was able to speak with clarity when Jesus asked them Who they believed Him to be.

Still, all of them followed Him wherever He led - even up Mt Calvary and all of them, except John, followed Him to an early death.

And knowing all this, Jesus still calls Peter out and says “Ye of little Faith.”

Many saints and great preachers have preached about the imagery in this story.

The water, for Jews at least, symbolized the chaos of the world. In the moment of creation, God separated the waters and brought order. Storms symbolized chaos, death, and the abyss.

The boat is the Church. Why? Because Peter is the rock upon which the Church is built and he is strongly associated with boats. Peter is a fisherman. Jesus stood on Peter’s boat to preach. Jesus revealed Himself to the apostles after His resurrection in the context of a boat. The boat is the Church.

When Peter - a man of great faith - pilots the boat through the storm, all is well. But when Peter steps out of the boat - he is immediately subject to chaos beyond his control. Jesus is Lord! But Peter’s personal strength is NOTHING without the stability of the Church.

The imagery strongly supports this interpretation. Why? Because Peter’s fault is met with Jesus’ hand AND HIS CRITICISM. And immediately, they return to the boat and the chaotic storm dies down. And immediately, everyone recognizes Jesus as He is - the Son of God. Peter tried to go it alone, but his personal faith failed him. He didn’t have what it takes to resist the world without the boat - without the Church. The shared faith of the Church, though, is entirely sufficient to calm the storm. When Peter is back in the boat, the whole Church resounds with Praise that Jesus is the son of God.

The reason that we go to confession, for example, and not just confess our sins to the Lord in private - is that WE are part of the Church. And so any separation between us and the Church, even personal sin, is detrimental to the whole Church.

My personal sin isn’t just a sin against the Lord, it’s also a sin against each of you. If I love the Lord with all my heart, soul, mind, and strength and my neighbor as myself, then I’m simply doing what I’m commanded to do. If I fail to love the Lord, then BY THAT FACT, I am failing to love you - my neighbor. I’m setting a bad example. But even if my sin is entirely private, I’m also failing to be present to the Holy Spirit and so I’m depriving you of a way in which the Holy Spirit might speak to you!

None of us is going to have Faith greater than Peter. And if he couldn’t make his way to Jesus separate from the Church, neither will we. Jesus called Peter to come to Him to prove that very point! Peter was called because he asked to be called and Jesus gave Him what he wanted. Thank God Jesus doesn’t answer all our prayers!

The overwhelming message of the Gospel is that our personal faith - on its own - is insufficient. On our own, we are OF LITTLE FAITH. But in the boat - within the Church - our shared faith protects us and delivers us.
