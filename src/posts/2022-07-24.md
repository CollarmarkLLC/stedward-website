## From The Font

_“If you, who are wicked, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him?"_

In our modern world of fragile egos, this can come across as harsh. But for people with real world experience, this is a powerful image. All of us have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God and all of us are chock full of what we call in theology “concupiscence,” which is the desire to sin. All of those distractions and those moments of self-interest… All of those times we are tempted not by devils, but by ourselves… If we are honest, each of us is a bit of a mess a lot of the time!

And yet look at the wondrous good that we’re still able to do! Look at how we’re able to love and to sacrifice and to create! Of course we know that the Lord is the one giving us all that potential and power, but look at what good God brings from broken vessels like us. It’s astounding. And as we think about it, we can’t help but marvel at God’s generosity and His overwhelming constancy for us!

Of course, our experience of God is most of what we know about Him and that can be a problem because our experiences are limited and colored by our concupiscence. The exception is the teaching of Jesus which is given to us as truth uncolored by our personal experience of the Lord.

And that’s what Jesus is very aware of as He preaches this sermon. He wants us to realize that God isn’t limited in the ways we are and so we should be fearless and persistent in asking for any- and everything we need and\or want. The Lord knows what is good for us and what isn’t. Just as a good parent forbids a child sweets right before supper, the Lord won’t give us what is detrimental to us. But that shouldn’t stop the child from the asking. After all, if the child expresses their hunger, then mom is able to satiate it with something even better that what they were asking for in the first place!

God knows us better than we know ourselves and He is very specific in telling us to ask, seek, and knock with every need and want that enters our hearts!

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

July 26 is the feast of Ss. Joachim and Ann, the Grandparents of Jesus and the patrons of ALL GRANDPARENTS!

As transportation and communication have developed, blood relationships have become less important to our sense of personal identity. For better or worse, most modern people know little to nothing about their ancestry or heritage beyond their grandparents.

And that’s a major part of the reason that grandparents matter more now than ever before! Our younger generations are lost in their own narcissism and so the sense of perspective, especially historical perspective, are such an important blessing for them!

Beyond anything so mundane as usefulness, grandparents are also just awesome! They know all the best stories about what mom and dad were like when they were younger… They know the rank hypocrisy (haha) that dad is showing when he punishes me for something that he did ten times!!! They are generally responsible for trying to spoil children to the constant chagrin of mom and dad… In sum, grandparents are a blessing to have!

I grew up with my dad’s parents around frequently and my dad’s dad is still kicking at the ripe old age of 98. My mom’s people are from Michigan (yes, I’ve got some Yankee blood, but it’s limited) and so I only got to see them once a year or so, but almost always around Christmas. My mom’s dad was a mechanic (who spoke just like Public Radio’s Car Talk) and he was the very definition of blue collar. “Poppy Frank” was Brusque, vulgar, and a little mean but that was in contrast to my dad’s dad “Pawpaw” who is a deeply cerebral man of few words. My mom’s mom was a carbon copy of Edith from All in the Family down to the voice and mannerisms! I’m not exaggerating at all.

When we visited my mom’s family, everything was loud and raucous and busy and chaotic. I don’t know that I would’ve liked it all the time, but it was wonderful as a kid. There was always snow and Christmas treats and a herd of cousins that I only ever saw at Christmas. It’s interesting that my mom is the quieter one of my parents.

When we went to my dad’s family, everything was much more subdued and peaceful. Visits to “pawpaw and nanny’s” place were all about the Grand Ole’ Opry and watching my mom and dad drink coffee that was clearly unappealing to them!

For a lot of us here, our extended families aren’t at a distance. But for some of us, they are. Either way, grandparents can have a profound impact upon kids. They can provide perspective, depth, and even context for the questions that arise in young minds, especially the questions that kids don’t feel comfortable discussing with their parents!

And while grandparents can be a pain in the keester sometimes, they are almost always a blessing to their grandkids. And so to all our grandparents on their patronal feast this Tuesday: We love you!, we need you!, and we’re praying for you!

PRAYER FOR HURRICANE SEASON… O God, Master of this passing world, hear the humble voices of your children. The Sea of Galilee obeyed your order and returned to its former quietude; you are still the Master of land and sea. We live in the shadow of a danger over which we have no control. The Gulf, like a provoked and angry giant, can awake from its seeming lethargy, overstep its conventional boundaries, invade our land and spread chaos and disaster. During this hurricane season, we turn to You, O loving Father. Spare us from past tragedies whose memories are still so vivid and whose wounds seem to refuse to heal with the passing of time. O Virgin, Star of the Sea, Our Beloved Mother, we ask you to plead with your Son in our behalf so that spared from the calamities common to this area and animated with a true spirit of gratitude, we will walk in the footsteps of your Divine Son to reach the heavenly Jerusalem where a stormless eternity awaits us. Amen (Fr. Al Volpe, Cameron Parish, LA, originally dedicated to the victims of Hurricane Audrey in 1957)
