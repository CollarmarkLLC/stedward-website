## From The Font

“When He comes, the Spirit of truth, He will guide you to all truth.”

Until the mid-1500s, it was universally accepted that statements which are not about taste are either true or false. I may like to eat tomatoes or not, but it is either true or false that they are fruits… But in that dark era that we wrongly call the Enlightenment, a small group of shockingly arrogant philosophers began the work of undermining the concept of “truth.” Nowadays, Friedrich Nietzsche’s ideas have become universally accepted and truth has lost most of its meaning. Now maybe I’m being too harsh… The moderns weren’t the only bad philosophers, they just happen to be the ones causing us the most trouble now.

Truth isn’t just a word or a philosophy… Jesus identifies His very nature with Truth! He says, “I AM the way, the truth, and the life.” And that “the truth will set [us] free!” In today’s Gospel, He also identifies the very nature of the Holy Spirit with the idea of truth. And so it’s not just something we can bemoan and set aside… The truth is essential to our Faith. And if we aren’t careful about thinking rightly, our Faith can be undermined by bad philosophy.

If we, for example, buy into Darwin’s idea that everything is just dumb luck and random chance, we’re going to have a hard time trusting that God has a plan. If we buy into the idea that truth isn’t absolute, we’re going to have a hard time trusting that God is Who He says He is… If there’s no absolute truth, then does that mean that Buddhism or Scientology is just as real as Christianity? If there’s no absolute truth, then why give of ourselves to the Lord? Why care? Why not just have as much pleasure as possible? Why not make myself the center of my life?

On the other hand, if there is absolute truth, then God is real and Heaven is real and I don’t get to decide who God is and which of His teachings I’ll follow… I don’t get to invent my own path to Heaven. In fact, logic and reason push me toward the Supernatural if I think rightly about truth…

Perhaps that’s why there’s so much effort being made to undermine our right thinking…

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

My first Mass was on Trinity Sunday in 2005. It’s typical for a new priest to invite a wisened mentor to preach at his first Mass. But the wisened mentor that I would have chosen, Fr. Stan Klores, was already slated to preach the first Mass of my classmate Fr. Luke Melcher at the Cathedral less than half an hour after my first Mass ended… As I considered someone else, I realized that my family - most of whom lived away from Alexandria - wouldn’t hear me preach often or, in the case of more distant relatives - ever. So I made the unusual decision to preach at my own first Mass.

I remember the moment fondly in part because I made three copies of the sermon (just in case) and two of them got lost between the opening hymn and the end of the Gospel… The sermon wasn’t on some grand theological topic on which I would have enjoyed pontificating. I preached that Sunday at Our Lady of Prompt Succor on the family and the way in which the nature of the family is to be a living icon of the Holy Trinity.

I’ve always been a very practical person. I like the high falutin theology and philosophy, but when it comes to living the Catholic Faith, most people don’t find it helpful or useful. And so in preaching, I almost always err on the side of practical and tangible advice rather than theoretical or even spiritual wisdom.

For most people, this is genuinely helpful! It gives us something to do. It doesn’t come across as incomprehensible word salad - after all, what does it practically mean when we say that Jesus is “consubstantial with the Father?” But there is a certain downside to emphasizing the practical at the expense of the spiritual and the ethereal: “Faith is the hope of things unseen” and “the foolishness of God confounds the wisest of the wise.” In sum, God isn’t practical. Prayer and the spiritual life aren’t practical. Much of our Faith isn’t even about “doing” so much as it is about “being” and having an attitude and a disposition of love for God! And we all know that love is anything but practical.

I have kept one and only one of my New Years resolutions this year… I resolved to preach more about Jesus this year. And that’s forced me to re-evaluate what my sermons look like. If I’m going to talk about Jesus, Himself, there’s going to be less practical wisdom, less concrete advice, less obvious value in the sermons.

At the same time, to know the Lord Jesus is to love Him and love overwhelms all the practical wisdom in the world…

So far this year, most of the Sundays have been locked into themes related to Christmas, Lent and Easter. But now that we’re beginning Ordinary Time, the opportunities to wander a little from the readings will afford us some freedom to really think about Jesus, Himself. We’ll have some time to ask Who it is preaching about the Kingdom of God and What the words of Jesus have to say about the Man who is God.

I hope that I’ll be able to carve a good path between the practical and the obtuse! That said, I would genuinely welcome feedback on my preaching or any aspect of my ministry which is helpful or not to you spiritually… Let’s help each other grow nearer to our Lord!

PRAYER FOR HURRICANE SEASON… O God, Master of this passing world, hear the humble voices of your children. The Sea of Galilee obeyed your order and returned to its former quietude; you are still the Master of land and sea. We live in the shadow of a danger over which we have no control. The Gulf, like a provoked and angry giant, can awake from its seeming lethargy, overstep its conventional boundaries, invade our land and spread chaos and disaster. During this hurricane season, we turn to You, O loving Father. Spare us from past tragedies whose memories are still so vivid and whose wounds seem to refuse to heal with the passing of time. O Virgin, Star of the Sea, Our Beloved Mother, we ask you to plead with your Son in our behalf so that spared from the calamities common to this area and animated with a true spirit of gratitude, we will walk in the footsteps of your Divine Son to reach the heavenly Jerusalem where a stormless eternity awaits us. Amen (Fr. Al Volpe, Cameron Parish, LA, originally dedicated to the victims of Hurricane Audrey in 1957)
