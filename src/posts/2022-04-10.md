## From The Font

_“Now one of the criminals hanging there reviled Jesus, saying, ‘Are you not the Christ? Save yourself and us.’”_

Prior to Jesus, when basically anyone on the planet asked to be saved, they were asking for rescue from a physical threat. Jewish and Greek ideas about the afterlife were bleak and philosophical rather than personal rewards from God. Before Jesus, saving someone meant saving their life.

It’s no accident that Jesus chose to die the way He did. His very public, very painful death was a clear message to everyone that the universe is not how they thought it was. Even these words from the thief highlight how wrong the old way of thinking really was. The thief asks to be saved but Jesus denies him physical relief. The other thief, though, asks not to be saved but that Jesus remembers him… This, too, is wrong, in part. It comes from the Greek way of thinking about memory and memorializing the dead. You may remember the first few chapters of The Iliad go into this kind of thinking about the afterlife. Jesus isn’t going to save the thief who wants physical salvation and He’s not going to merely remember the other thief who wants a kind of Greek memorialization. Jesus is going to offer a new category of saving: an eternal, personal Heaven which is a perfect union with the Lord God. It’s not a super-charged version of Earth with extra-potent bodily pleasures. It’s not a merely intellectual or philosophical place as the Buddhist or Taoists believe. Heaven is a perfect union with Jesus.

We experience a foreshadowing of Heaven in this life when we feel timeless moments with those we love. When we feel perfectly at peace… When we feel totally at home in a relationship… When we recognize how blessed we are to have so-and-so in our lives, we are getting a taste of Heavenly bliss.

So it should make sense that even horrific suffering in this life is a small price to pay for eternal life in Heaven with Jesus! In our own relationships, we understand that sacrifice and compromise is worth it for the joy of friendship and intimacy… How much more is any trial in this life worth it for eternal, perfect union with the Lord.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

I don’t know about you, dear reader, but I can’t believe that Holy Week is already here! I know some of y’all have said that Lent felt extra-long this year, but, for me, it’s been unusually short.

I suppose, that’s because I’ve had so many out of the ordinary events this Lent. While I used to preach a lot of out-of-town missions, since I’ve been a pastor (~12 years), that’s been harder and harder to make time to do. This year, I preached two extended parish missions and a day retreat for Confirmation kids. The missions were deeply fulfilling at a personal level because they required a very different set of intellectual, religious, and spiritual perspectives. I told the folks at both missions that I love being here in Tallulah. And I Do!! In 17 years of priesthood, this is the first time that I’ve been out of “fix it” mode and in “keep it running smoothly” mode… It’s wonderful! Still, the missions afforded me an opportunity to switch modes for just a few days and to preach on some topics from a different perspective than I would use here in Tallulah.

While those two weeks of parish Lenten missions were exhausting, they were also invigorating. I’m so thankful to be able to bring that energy back here to Tallulah especially in light of the struggles we’re facing as a community… Our disordered national, state and regional leadership, those beloved parishioners suffering from serious illness, the remainder of COVID, the ongoing financial crises, and, of course, the storm damage to TA are all weighing on us to the point that we might rightly ask whether we needed to do much extra penance in order for Lent to do its work in our hearts! We might also ask how we’ll be able to shift gears into rejoicing in Easter glory in only a week’s time…

I shared these thoughts about these missions because they illustrate our human need to shift gears from time to time. We need to step out of our ruts from time to time. Lent makes us think about our eating patterns and our personal spiritual program. Easter reminds us to set aside the weight of the world for a bit and simply rejoice that God is God and that Heaven is our real purpose in this life. Even Sunday Mass is a weekly moment to reorient ourselves toward the Lord and realize that the daily grind doesn’t define us. So, too, our daily prayers offer us that same freedom…

I was sharing with someone this week how shifting gears can be difficult, especially shifting into a new gear. The first time I tried Sushi, for example, I just about barfed! Bait soaked in salty brown water (soy sauce). It was so nasty. There was a second try only because people I trusted said it would be worth it. Now, Sushi is one of my very favorite foods. We see the same pattern with prayer. At first, it’s tedious. But if we give it a chance, it provides a freedom from the daily grind and the weight of the world that we all need so desperately!

Shake ups can be hard, but God makes all things work for the good of those who love Him. Holy Week is already here, but the value of that gear shifting isn’t diminished… Let’s make the best of the coming days!
