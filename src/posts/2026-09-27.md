## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

September is traditionally devoted to Our Lady of Sorrows. That’s something that all Catholics used to know just like we know that Christmas is at the end of December.

It’s important to know about Our Lady of Sorrows. That’s the title of the Blessed Virgin Mary which is associated with her sufferings - first when She was told She would suffer by Simeon in the temple, then when She had to flee to Egypt, then when She & Joseph lost Jesus and found Him in the temple, then when She watched his crucifixion and death and finally when she went with another Joseph (of Arimathaea) to bury him in the tomb. All of that suffering - from the moment Jesus was born until after He died - Mary endured it. She suffered so much.

Nowadays, even Christians look at suffering as if it were something to be avoided (if you can) or minimized (if you can’t). Most of us see suffering as bad - even evil.

The reality is that suffering allows us to recognize our own faults. It allows us to see this world as it is. It provides clarity. It reveals us to ourselves. We can’t know someone and we can’t know ourselves until we face adversity, struggle, or emotional hardship.

And so, why do Christians care about being there for the sick and the dying? Because that moment of misery can be the most important moment of one’s life! Suffering is not good in itself, but it creates a way of understanding that can’t be found in a life of constant ease and luxury.

If any of us went through our entire lives without suffering at all and we died and we stood before the throne of God and He looked at us and offered us forgiveness for our sins... We would laugh in His face. “My life was perfect. Everything went my way. Practically speaking, I’m as much a god as you are... I don’t need anything from you! Everything already went my way.”

Suffering cuts through lies. It cuts through pride. It cuts through selfishness and grounds us in reality.

The little book that came out a few years ago called Tuesdays with Maury revealed this truth to a lot of people. It tells the story of a man who visited an old teacher who was dying of cancer. He went to see him every week for months as the old man got worse and worse. As he lost more and more, the teacher got wiser and more reflective. As he lost his ability to walk and then to bathe himself and then to get out of bed - the author couldn’t help but realize how meaningless things like TV or bickering or gossip really are. Who cares if uncle Fred cussed aunt Emmy fifty years ago...

Suffering reveals so much and that’s why Jesus chose to save the world by suffering. He could’ve wiggled his nose like Samatha on Bewitched. He could’ve snapped his fingers... He could’ve simply said a word like He did at the creation of the world in Genesis 1:1.

With all the power that this world can imagine, God chose to become a person and to suffer to redeem the world. His suffering gives our suffering meaning and power. Yes it still hurts. It’s still scary. It always will be. But as human beings, we need that.

Suffering is not something to avoid - it’s something to embrace and to thank God for even as you ask Him to take away the hardest parts. Jesus did that. In Gethsemane, He asked God to take the cup of suffering away from Him. But He added, let your will be done. St. Paul also asked that God would take away the thorn in his flesh - that one sin that he just couldn’t kick. As a priest, I ask God to make parts of my life easier, but I trust in the power of unanswered prayers and embrace whatever cross the Lord asks me to carry!

It’s ok to want to avoid suffering. It’s ok to ask God to keep you and your loved ones from suffering. It’s ok to ask God for an easy and blessed life. But if God loves us - He’s going to want us to get to heaven and to do that we’re going to need to suffer in this life. So when it comes, we embrace it. We ask the Holy Spirit for help. We ask the Church for help. But we embrace it and trust that God is using those trials and difficulties to open for us the doors to Heaven where there is no suffering - precisely because it is no longer needed!
