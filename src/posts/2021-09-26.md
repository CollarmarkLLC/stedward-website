## From The Font

_“For whoever is not against us is for us.”_

Plenty of people have pointed out that this simple aphorism doesn’t at all jive with Mt 12:30 where Jesus says ‘whoever is not with me is against me.”

In reality, there’s a lot of context and language barriers, but the essential problem isn’t about these contrasting verses, it’s about the idea that the Bible should be read in this way at all.

The Bible isn’t written as one integrated book with one clear storyline and one clear main character’s point of view and one unseen, all-wise editor bringing everything together in one style. The Bible wasn’t even written by people with a shared purpose… Some books were written to instruct, but others were written primarily to preserve historical details or tribal wisdom. Some were written to be sung or prayed or performed while others, while others were written to be studied only by legal experts. Some try to convert people to Judaism or Christianity, while others are written to chide or correct religious communities already firmly established.

The modern Protestant understandings of the Bible as “Basic Instruction Before Leaving Earth” or as “Life’s Instruction Booklet” or as “God’s definitive word” are simply wrong. What’s more, they’re stupid, by which I mean irrational. These understandings are just made up out of thin air. None of them come from the Bible itself… None of them come from the wisdom of the Holy Spirit expressed through History (which we call Sacred Tradition)... Ultimately, none of them even come from human reason. They’re all just made up because some preacher or atheist or whoever decided that their personal beliefs required the Bible to be this or that way.

In reality, the so-called contradictions of the Bible arise only when someone over-focuses on some detail to the exclusion of the whole religious picture. Understood as a cohesive religious statement, the Bible is integral and complete. But when we over-focus on why the Hornets in Revelation have Blond Hair, we get nonsense about the inherent sexism in the early Church…

A proper reading of the Bible, as the saints have attested from the beginning, begins and ends on one’s knees - that is, in prayer.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

This Wednesday is Michaelmas - the feast of St. Michael the Archangel. In the 60s, Ss. Gabriel and Raphael were robbed of their feast days and huddled together with poor Michael so that now the Archangels share a single day of celebration.

The angels are an increasingly important part of our faith as, increasingly, we are encouraged by the world to ignore or reject the supernaturality of Christianity. The angels are fascinating theologically as well because the way they are structured is easy to understand (even if it’s really, really hard to dig into why they are structured that way).

The first thing to know is that each angel is as different from one other as a dolphin is from a paperclip. In theology, we say that “each angel is its own distinct ‘species’”.

From there, we find in Scripture, nine broad purposes for which these multitudes of differing angel species are created. St. Thomas Aquinas labeled these divisions-by-purpose as Choirs. St. Thomas uses a lot of words that seem odd to us… there are good reasons for his word choices, so don’t fret if they sound weird.

The nine “choirs” of angels are then divided into three tiers of three choirs each. The top tier consists of angels who are created by God for purposes in Heaven and around God’s throne. The bottom tier consists of angels who are created by God for purposes around us human beings. The middle tier, then, fills in the gaps between Heaven and us dealing with the universe at large.

At the top of the top are the Seraphim. They exist to worship at God’s Throne constantly.

Next come the Cherubim, who are NOT fat babies and who do NOT shoot little arrows. They also exist to worship God but in a broader, across the entirety of Heaven and not just at the Throne. These are also the angels who worship the Holy Eucharist wherever it is reserved.

The Thrones function as liaisons between the angels working outside of Heaven and the Glory of God in Heaven.

The Dominions, Virtues, and Powers are the middle tier angels at work in the Universe at large…

The top of the bottom, so to speak, are the Principalities which are angels who function like guardian angels but for who cities or regions. Lots of exorcists have lots to say about these.

The bottom of the bottom - closest to us - are the Angels (aka “simple angels), who help us most directly.

The Archangels - who are the eighth choir - are a little complicated, mostly because we tend to think of them as the biggest, best, greatest, strongest, etc… But it’s not really that simple. The Archangels are a big deal and Lucifer (aka Satan) is an Archangel and He was the most beautiful of the angels… Like I said, it’s not 100% easy to comprehend the ins and outs, but the overall framework fits into this short article.

More to come when we get to the Guardian angels next week!
