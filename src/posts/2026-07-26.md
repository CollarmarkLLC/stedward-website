## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Christianity is hard. It’s not like Buddhism or Judaism or even Islam. Christianity is hard because it really is all or nothing.
Jesus makes some wildly unreasonable claims about Himself. And those claims are either the ravings of a lunatic or the revelation of a God.

If they are the ravings of a lunatic - then the whole story of Christianity is nonsense. But if they are the revelation of a God, then the WHOLE story of Christianity is non-negotiable. We are supposed to go to everyone in the world and convert them to Catholicism. We are supposed to lay down our lives for Jesus. We are to sell everything we have and buy the pearl of great price - figuratively and spiritually. You either trade it all for Jesus or you don’t get any payout at all.

We are one of the first generations in human history to have a problem with this kind of thinking.

It wasn’t until 1968 that Catholics started to believe - wrongly - that we could pick and choose what we want to believe and not believe. It was when Pope Paul VI wrote a simple document saying that what the Church has always believed, She still believes. It shouldn’t have been controversial. But, a lot of “experts” - priests, bishops and college professors included - had spent the previous decade writing that Vatican II meant everything was up for grabs and that every one of us was supposed to make it up for ourselves. It was a kind of idolatry with this caricature of “conscience” as the golden calf.

The Gospel story for today of the pearl of great price, though, is a rejection of that kind of pick- and-choose way of thinking. We don’t get to just follow our conscience and we don’t get to pick-and- choose our expert or ideology. The pearl is what it is. I can buy it or not, but I can’t change it.

The Lord says that when we truly give ourselves to Faith, when we trade everything, we find peace and joy and kindness and self-possession.

When Jesus offers us the pearl of great price, He says there will be costs. He says that it won’t be easy. He says that it’s no accident that it’s hard - it’s meant to be hard. We’re meant to struggle. Jesus chose the Cross. He chose the crown of thorns. But St. Paul says “We know that all things work for good for those who love God.” Even the hard ones…

The cross He has asked me to carry is exactly what I need to get to heaven. It’s what is perfectly suited to me and my personality and my spiritual gifts and my failings. It was planned for me when I was knit together in my mother’s womb!

And if I devote myself to “taking up my cross and following after Him” and to “working out my salvation in fear and trembling” and to “hearing the word of God and keeping it” then I will find a depth of satisfaction and a nearness to God and a desire for heaven that far surpasses anything this world can offer! This is the pearl - a stone which starts as a painful grain of sand in the innards of an oyster.

None of this is about devoting myself entirely to a Faith that is foreign to us. Our entire culture comes from Christianity. Our notions of family and honesty and goodness and hospitality come from Christians imperfectly trying to live out today’s Gospel.

And we can see that where Christianity is being suppressed how quickly the culture dissolves away - just look at England and France and California.

And why is it getting suppressed? Because we intuit and know that pick-and-choose Christianity is fake and unsatisfying. Jesus says that the Kingdom of God IS like the pearl of great price. Either you buy in 100% or you might as well just cash out.

Getting the pearl takes more than words and token actions, it takes courageous, directed, long-term and serious effort. That is what God wants from us and for us!

And his promises - now, in the course of our lives and in eternity - make for a very compelling deal.

As the infomercials of the 90s always asked, what are you waiting for?
