## From The Font

_“...In those days...”_

Prophecy is a funny thing… As Christians, we definitely believe that God has a plan which He has been affecting through all of history. From the so-called Protogospel in Genesis 3, Jesus has been foreshadowed and not only the Incarnation of Jesus, but His second coming and the end of this creation…

Still, foreshadowing isn’t meaningful only in light of its fulfillment. The Christmas prophecy of the Virgin who will bear a child named Emmanuel doesn’t ONLY apply to Jesus. In fact, that verse is meaningful in all sorts of ways. Bible Study 101 reminds us that there are both literal and spiritual meanings of most every verse in the Bible. That applies as much to prophecies as it does to simple morals.

And so prophecy is a funny thing. Whenever we come across a phrase like “in those days,” the Bible is usually pulling out of the ordinary flow of time and calling to think about time and space and history in a very different way. Whether it’s the end of days that Jesus points to in the Gospel or the unknown future time of the Emmanuel prophecy or even the odd way that Daniel uses it in our first reading. Daniel, in particular, isn’t pointing to some radically different date. He’s talking about his own lifetime. But he’s thinking about time very differently than we usually do. He’s not saying “in 2016 by the Julian Calendar this or that will happen.” It’s more like the beginning of Revelation where St John is in a trance and feels far away in time and space from this moment. “In those days… in that time… in that frame of mind… I saw these things.”

The reason we care about little details like this boils down to the biblical equivalent of social cues. None of us reads Hebrew or Greek fluently. And so it’s VERY hard to get the sense of all the different tenses and literary techniques that give language its subtlety. These three little words - “In those days” - are a context clue which fills in the gap between Koine Greek with all its tenses and modern English with its relative lack of nuance. A single Greek word ending can imply that the verb of the sentence began long ago and then ended and then unexpectedly began again. That’s hard to translate! With just a few context clues, we can help ourselves out a lot.

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Did you know that clocks actually move slower as the effects of gravity decrease? It’s not just the clock, it’s time itself. Time moves slower at high altitudes… It seems insane, but it was proven 100% true when the GPS system was being perfected… It’s bizarre.

Both the philosophical understanding and practical applications of time are hard for us because we are stuck inside of the question we’re asking. It’s hard the way that psychology and sociology and even politics are hard.

It’s the question of time that makes Heaven, Hell, and Purgatory hard to get our heads around. Heaven and Hell are not “ever-lasting,” they are “eternal.” “Ever-lasting” means that something goes on for infinitely many days - sort of like taxes… “Eternal” means that something exists separate from any sense of time passing - sort of like the experience of deep love. Heaven and Hell both exist eternally without any sense of time. Without time, things don’t occur one after another… There’s no possibility of change. Without time, there’s no series of events or new knowledge or understanding. There’s only the perfection of our union with (or separate from) God’s perfect love\peace\joy (which are indistinguishable from one another in eternity).

Purgatory, though, is different. Purgatory is a process and a process means change and development and a sense of learning. Processes exist in a temporal flow. It’s certainly not the same as our sense of time here and now, but it is a sense of some sort of time.

Perhaps your starting to see why this whole time thing is hard to get our heads around! The discoveries about time of Albert Einstein (which are called “time dilation” if you’re interested) actually provide a little help in understanding the philosophy of time. In the most common sense understanding of time: ...one thing happens after another in a fixed way... ...our perception of that change (work seems to last forever which a good day at the lake is over all too soon) is just a meaningless oddity… In reality, though, time itself is not just a function of perception, but a physical part of the universe. Remember that if this were false, GPS wouldn’t work… so there’s a certainty to this way of thinking.

As a spiritual parallel, the version of time which exists in Purgatory is not merely a perception or an intellectual exercise of Catholics, but something which we would expect and predict based upon the rational and the dogmatic aspects of our Faith.

God hasn’t chosen to reveal any more detail about Purgatory than its existence and the nature of time-flow within it. But, really, that’s enough for us to get a basic idea of what it is and why it is.

Just as time in this life is a genuine function of God’s love and mercy, so time in Purgatory is a genuine function of God’s love and mercy. The absence of time in Heaven also speaks of God’s love, but not so much of His merciful love as of His eternal, perfection of love. Of course, there’s more to say, but maybe this will serve as food for thought and prayer in this month of prayer for our Dearly Departed.
