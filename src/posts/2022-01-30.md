## From The Font

_“Before I formed you in the womb I knew you.”_

These words from the prophecy of Jeremiah form perhaps the most humanly intimate and consoling image in all of scripture. God tells us that He personally formed each of us in the wombs of our individual mothers from an idea so perfectly loved and so intimately understood in God’s mind that the idea cannot be distinguished from our very souls. It’s one of the deepest and most profound statements about the relationship between flesh and spirit that exists in any religious text or writing in the world and at the same time, it’s immediately understandable and visceral. God’s knowledge of me is my soul. My soul is indistinguishable from God’s love for me and His plan for me and His mercy for me. I exist, flesh and spirit, because God maintains me in His Divine Mind.

Philosophy can be very off-putting. It can seem meaninglessly complex and unnecessary. We tend to think of it as the intellectual rambling of self-important people. And, trust me - one of my degrees is in philosophy - lots of philosophy is exactly the intellectual rambling of self- important people. But philosophy is really just a way of cataloging ideas so that we can understand how things and ways of thinking are connected. Theology works in much the same way.

The philosophy which underlies these simple words from Jeremiah can be expressed with insider lingo like ‘ontology’ and ‘hylomorphism’ or it can be understood in very immediate and very human images like that of a kindly Father knitting together a child in a mother’s womb.

While both the complex and the intimate have their place in the Church and in getting people to Heaven, there’s a wisdom in seeking out those biblical images that speak to you personally. The image of God forming you in your mother’s womb probably moves you. So might other passages about God’s concern for us, Jesus’ love for us, the Holy Spirit’s activity among us, Mary’s tenderness, Paul’s courage, Peter’s human foibles, Jonah’s nerves, Job’s steadfastness, Elijah’s intensity, Daniel’s Mic drops or John’s nickname - “Beloved Disciple” - which is there to allow us to insert ourselves into the story. If it moves you, stick with it! The Lord gave us the Bible for good reasons!

## Thoughts from Fr. Ryan

Our first month of workshops on Dealing with Stress and Anxiety as a Christian was wildly successful. Nearly one third of our parish attended one of the two talks I gave which functioned as an overview of the way that our brains process stress and the specific tools that our Faith give us to deal with it: Prayer, Knowledge of God, Knowledge of Self, Penance & Fasting, Works of Mercy, and the Christian Community.

This Tuesday, Shawn Wheat, a professor of Nursing at NSU in Natchitoches will be traveling in to address misconceptions, myths and errors that cause us unneeded stress and anxiety. Shawn is a remarkable woman and an incredible Catholic. She grew up in New Orleans and has spent most of her adult life in Natchitoches where she works at the college. Her husband is a practicing psychiatrist with years of in-the-field experience. Shawn is also the proud and loving mother of two kids. She and I have also been in a book club together for over ten years, so it’s safe to say that I trust her completely.

As many of you know, this topic of mental health is near and dear to my heart. It’s a serious source of problems for many of us here in Tallulah and here at St. Edward in particular. But it doesn’t have to be! So many of our problems come not from the actual stressors in our lives, but from our unwillingness to face them and deal with them. This month’s presentation, in particular, is one that Catholics struggle with and which can and should be both enlightening and freeing to hear!

The presentation will be in the parish hall on Tuesday, February 1, 2022 at 6pm. It is, of course, free and you’re welcome to invite anyone you’d like, regardless of their Church affiliation.

Several of you have asked me about the recording of the previous talk. Live recording of my presentation did not go very well. It was just too much to try and do on my own. As such, I’m in the process of recording that talk in my office, so that it can be easily understood and so that the slides are easy to see. It’s a learning process, but it should be completed in a week or two. I’m also reworking the parish website to make the videos easier to find and to watch. In the meantime, I ask and recommend your prayers for those in need:

> _Lord, our God, you graciously chose St. Dymphna as patroness of those afflicted with mental and nervous disorders. She is thus an inspiration and a symbol of charity to the thousands who ask for her intercession. Please grant, Lord, through the prayers of this pure youthful martyr, relief and consolation to all suffering such trials, and especially those for whom we pray._

> _We beg you, Lord, to hear the prayers of St. Dymphna on our behalf. Grant all those for whom we pray patience in their sufferings and resignation to your divine will. Please fill them with hope, and grant them the relief and cure they so much desire. We ask this through Christ our Lord who suffered agony in the garden._

> _Amen_
